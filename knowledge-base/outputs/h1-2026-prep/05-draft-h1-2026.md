## 1. Introduction & Executive Summary — *REFRESH*

**New lead framing (the report card):**

> *Six months ago we told you residential would stay muted until rates eased, that tariffs were the cost threat to watch, that infrastructure would hold, and that M&A would stay hot. Three of those four held. What we got wrong — and what nobody on our distribution list was sizing correctly — was the timing of the housing turn and the single biggest demand story of the half: the data-center build-out. Here is what held, what didn't, and what it means for the businesses we advise.*

**Performance & Outlook.** H1 2026 was a half of divergence. Residential construction stayed weak and choppy — a March head-fake (starts +10.8%) gave way to an April decline and a softer-than-expected May. Nonresidential held up, but the engine was not the federal IIJA/CHIPS/IRA programs we emphasized last edition; it was a **private, AI-driven data-center construction boom** that pulled through HVAC, electrical, steel, concrete, and roofing demand. Material costs, which we flagged as the renewed threat, escalated harder than we forecast on tariffs — then partially whipsawed back mid-year. And the corporate story of the half was **consolidation**, capped by Berkshire Hathaway's $8.5B bet on homebuilder Taylor Morrison.

**Key Risks (H2 2026).**
- **Rate-gated residential recovery that keeps slipping — and may now reverse.** Mortgage rates stayed "higher and volatile," and the Fed's June 17 hold came with a hawkish turn under new chair Kevin Warsh (forward guidance dropped, inflation projection raised, dot plot tilted to a hike). The easing-into-recovery base case we ran last time did not arrive, and relief now looks like a 2027 story.
- **Tariff/policy whipsaw.** The cost line is now hostage to policy volatility, not just price trend — escalation through Q1, partial reversal in June. The *uncertainty itself* is the planning risk.
- **Demand concentration in data centers.** A real tailwind, but it concentrates exposure in a single, capital- and power-constrained, increasingly politically-contested demand source.
- **Affordability ceiling.** $440K median new-home price and soft confidence cap any organic residential rebound.

**Success Factors.** Cost/tariff mitigation and procurement agility; exposure (direct or pull-through) to data-center demand; balance-sheet strength to act in a consolidating market; and operational readiness for post-merger integration as the deal wave continues.

## 3. Market Context & Outlook — *REFRESH*

**H1 2026 Summary** (replaces "2025 YTD Summary"). The macro backdrop softened rather than firmed. Q4 2025 GDP was revised down sharply to **0.7%** (from a 1.4% advance), and the Atlanta Fed's GDPNow put Q1 2026 at **~1.6%**, with mainstream recession-probability estimates spanning **20–42%**. Consumer confidence cratered to a 12-year low of **84.5 in January** before edging back to **91.8 by March** — better, but still fragile and well below pre-cycle norms. Against that backdrop, building materials demand split cleanly: weak rate-sensitive residential, resilient nonresidential carried by digital infrastructure.

**Forward Outlook — H2 2026** (replaces the "EOY 2025 + 2026 Prospectus"). We carry the postmortem's lessons forward explicitly:
- **Residential recovery is delayed and rate-gated**, not cancelled — but after the hawkish June 17 hold the base case is now 2027, with a real chance rates rise before they fall. Don't anchor the timing to Fed-cut expectations again — we did that last time and it cost us the call.
- **Tariffs remain the swing factor on cost**, but the framing shifts from "prices rising" to "policy volatility" — plan for both directions.
- **Data centers are the growth engine** of nonresidential and the most reliable demand thread into H2.
- **Consolidation continues**, reshaping procurement leverage and channel access for every manufacturer client.

## 4. Drivers of Market Health — *REFRESH + NET-NEW*

Keep the ranked driver table and the per-driver **Impact / Implication** format. Editing calls per [Delta 03](03-lever-by-lever-delta.md): three levers are net-new rewrites, four are refreshes.

**① Interest & Mortgage Rates — NET-NEW (was: easing → recovery).** Our easing thesis failed. Rates stayed "higher and volatile" through H1; April new-home sales slumped on higher rates and prices. The June 17 FOMC capped the half: held at 3.50–3.75% (4th straight hold, 12–0), but Warsh's first meeting turned hawkish — forward guidance dropped, end-2026 PCE projection raised to 3.6%, dot-plot median up to ~3.6–4.1%, 9 of 18 projecting a hike. *Implication:* reframe around rate volatility and a hawkish hold; the recovery is now a 2027 case with rise-before-fall risk; stop forecasting off Fed-cut timing. ✅ Fed funds confirmed 3.50–3.75% (Jun 17); 30-yr fixed low-6%.

**② Labor — REFRESH+ (still tight, first cracks).** NAHB still names labor a binding constraint on starts; workforce-development pushes continue. But **ISM Services employment is contracting** — the first loosening signal, and exactly the wage-moderation watchpoint we flagged. *Implication:* "still tight, first cracks appearing."

**③ Material & Energy Costs — NET-NEW (lead lever; Neutral → clearly Negative).** The strongest call we made, and we *under*-called the magnitude. Construction PPI ran **+3.6% YoY** (largest since Jan 2023); aluminum mill shapes **+28% YoY** under a 50% tariff; nonferrous **+25%**; **53% of contractors** rank materials cost a top-3 concern. S&P Global dubbed it the "Year of the Tariff." Then a **mid-year partial reversal** — June "Trump tariff cuts," Mexico/Canada seeking steel relief in the USMCA review. *Implication:* track the escalation→reversal **whipsaw**; policy volatility is the headline risk. ⚠ Confirm latest PPI/lumber/steel/aluminum prints + reversal status before publication.

**④ Demand Visibility — NET-NEW (split the section).** *Residential:* choppy, resolving downward — March +10.8%, April −2.8%, May starts and permits both down more than expected. *Nonresidential:* held, but **data-center / AI-infrastructure construction became the dominant driver**, not the federal programs we emphasized. ABI **stabilized below the growth line** (Dec 48.5 → Jan 43.8 → Feb 49.4) — bottoming, not reversing. *Implication:* report residential as rate-gated chop and break out data-center-led nonres as its own thread (§5).

**⑤ Government Infrastructure Spending — REFRESH + reframe (Positive, reweighted).** $430B+ under IIJA/CHIPS/IRA remains the durable floor, but **private data-center capex eclipsed it** as the marginal driver of nonres demand. *Implication:* federal = floor; data centers = growth engine (cross-ref §5).

**⑥ Credit Availability — REFRESH (compress; unchanged).** Stayed restrictive for small/spec borrowers; large caps raised freely (CEMEX $3B sustainability-linked revolver, $1.5B notes); consumer-credit stress visible (LendingTree −22%). Lowest-change lever — compress to a short update, reuse last edition's framing.

**⑦ GDP & Consumer Confidence — REFRESH (gap persisted, now closing downward).** The divergence we flagged held, but GDP is now slowing *toward* the weak sentiment rather than the gap closing upward (Q4'25 0.7%, GDPNow Q1'26 1.6%, confidence 84.5→91.8). *Implication:* keep the "why GDP strength isn't felt" analysis box (it landed well); refresh figures.

## 5. NEW — Data-Center & AI-Infrastructure Demand — *NET-NEW (marquee demand story)*

The half's biggest demand surprise and our top sales hook. AI-driven data-center construction pulled demand through nearly every tracked segment: **thermal/HVAC** (Modine's **$4B** long-term capacity agreement through 2029; Trane's LiquidStack; JCI guiding to **1GW** of data-center thermal and a YORK 3.5MW-class unit; Carrier's AquaEdge chiller; AAON citing a **$1B** data-center opportunity), **construction/engineering** (Sterling Infrastructure as a pure-play data-center builder), **envelope and finishes** (Sherwin-Williams positioning as the single data-center coatings partner; USG/Subzero alliance; Kingspan), and a global pipeline (Nordic, APAC, West Texas, DFW immersion-cooled).

Why it matters for our clients: this is where nonresidential growth actually came from, and it has a multi-year runway. It also carries real constraints — **power availability, water use, and rising local political opposition** (Ohio suspended a data-center tax break; activist pushback in IL/TX/AZ). *Implication:* exposure to data-center demand — direct or pull-through — is the cleanest growth story in the sector, but it concentrates risk.
