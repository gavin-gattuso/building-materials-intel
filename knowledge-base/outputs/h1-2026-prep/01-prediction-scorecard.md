# Prediction Scorecard — 2025 YTD Report → H1 2026 Actuals

Every forward-looking call made in the **2025 YTD report (Nov 2025)** scored against what the KB recorded through June 2026. This is the raw material for the new **Postmortem / "What We Said vs. What Happened"** section.

**Verdict key:** ✅ Hit · 🟡 Partial · ❌ Miss · ⚠ Needs verification

| # | What we predicted (Nov 2025) | What actually happened (H1 2026) | Verdict | Implication going forward |
|---|------------------------------|----------------------------------|---------|---------------------------|
| 1 | **Rates ease → residential recovers.** "Rates are declining… gradual recovery through late 2025, strengthening into 2026 as quantitative easing takes hold." (Fed funds 3.75–4.00%, mortgage easing to 6.2–6.35%.) | Mortgage rates stayed **"higher and volatile"**; new-home sales slumped in April on higher rates/prices; recovery did **not** arrive on the predicted timeline. Tentative bottoming signs only emerged late H1. | ❌ **Miss** | Drop the "easing → recovery" base case. Reframe 2026 as a **delayed, rate-gated** recovery. Don't anchor forecasts to Fed-cut timing again. |
| 2 | **Single-family demand muted into 2026.** Weak permits/absorption; recovery only once borrowing costs improve. | Single-family starts **fell 2.8% in April to 1.47M SAAR** (NAHB/Census); affordability still a barrier ($440K median). But March starts surged 10.8% and "builders breaking ground at fastest pace in 2 years" — choppy, not a clean trend. | 🟡 **Partial** | "Muted" was right; the **timing of any turn** was the hard part. Lead with the chop, not a single direction. |
| 3 | **Tariffs are the renewed cost threat** — could add $7,500–$10,000 per new home (metals 20–25%, Canadian lumber 35%). | Materialized and then some: construction PPI **+3.6% YoY** (largest since Jan 2023); **aluminum mill shapes +28% YoY** under a 50% tariff; nonferrous +25%; **53% of contractors** rank materials cost a top-3 concern. Then a **mid-year partial reversal** ("Trump tariff cuts," Jun 4). | ✅ **Hit** (magnitude under-called) | Best call we made. Keep tariffs front-and-center but now track the **reversal/whipsaw** — policy volatility itself is the risk. |
| 4 | **Nonres resilient on IIJA/CHIPS/IRA.** Federal programs provide durable, multi-year demand. | Nonres did hold — **but the driver shifted.** The dominant H1 2026 story was the **data-center / AI-infrastructure boom** (Modine $4B capacity deal, Trane, JCI 1GW, Sterling Infrastructure, Amazon HVAC), not the federal programs we emphasized. | 🟡 **Partial** (right outcome, wrong engine) | **Biggest blind spot.** Add a dedicated data-center / AI-infrastructure thread this edition. This is also a prime sales hook. |
| 5 | **Labor stays a binding constraint;** watch for moderating wage inflation. | NAHB still cites labor shortages as a starts constraint; workforce-development pushes continue; ISM Services **employment contracting** (early sign of loosening). | ✅ **Hit** | Keep, but note the early loosening. Frame as "still tight, first cracks appearing." |
| 6 | **Credit stays restrictive** for small/spec borrowers; large caps still access capital. | Held: large caps raised freely (CEMEX $3B revolver, $1.5B notes); consumer-credit stress visible (LendingTree −22%). No dramatic loosening. | ✅ **Hit** | Low-change lever — candidate to **compress/refresh** rather than rewrite (see outline). |
| 7 | **GDP–sentiment gap persists;** weak confidence caps discretionary demand. | Persisted — affordability still dragging sentiment and adjacent sectors (freight). | ✅ **Hit** | Keep; refresh figures only. |
| 8 | **M&A / portfolio activity continues** (cited +24% YoY deal size). | **Accelerated sharply** — 2025 deal volume +30% (BGL); H1 2026 wave: QXO/TopBuild $17B, Home Depot/GMS $5.5B, Lowe's/FBM, Holcim's 15-deal plan, **Berkshire/Taylor Morrison $8.5B**. | ✅ **Hit** (intensified) | Promote M&A from a theme to a **named section + callout box** (see Digest 02). |
| 9 | **ABI watchpoint:** possible reversal of the 16-month decline; backlog moderation by early 2026. | ⚠ Not yet confirmed in pulled KB set. | ⚠ **Verify** | Pull current ABI before publication — confirm whether the reversal we flagged happened. |

## Scorecard summary (for the report's opening "report card")
- **Called correctly (✅):** tariffs/cost pressure, labor tightness, credit, GDP–sentiment gap, M&A activity — **5 of 9**
- **Partially right (🟡):** residential timing, nonres engine (data centers vs. federal programs) — **2 of 9**
- **Missed (❌):** the rates-driven residential recovery timeline — **1 of 9**
- **Open (⚠):** ABI reversal — **1 of 9**

**Honest headline for readers:** *"We got the direction right on cost, labor, and consolidation. We were early on the residential recovery, and we under-weighted the single biggest demand surprise of the half — data centers."* That candor is exactly the credibility the kickoff wanted the postmortem to buy.
