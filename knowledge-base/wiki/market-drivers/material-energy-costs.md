# Material & Energy Costs

## Current Levels (April–May 2026) — ESCALATING

### PPI / Input Prices
- **Nonresidential construction PPI: +2.2% MoM in March 2026 — largest one-month jump in 4 years** (AGC)
- **Q1 2026 annualized input price growth: 18%** (Construction Dive, Apr 14)
- Construction tariff cost escalation estimated at ~8% aggregate, with tariff-exposed trades running well above that band

### Metals (March 2026 YoY)
- **Steel mill products: +15.4% YoY** (+2.1% MoM)
- **Aluminum: +34.1% YoY**
- **Copper: +21.3% YoY**
- Section 232 tariffs reached **50%** on items mostly made of steel/aluminum (Apr 2 adjustment); derivatives carry 25%

### Energy
- **Crude petroleum: +20.2% MoM in March** — Iran war disrupted oil trade flows
- **Diesel fuel: +14% MoM** (March, near-record one-month jump)
- Brent crude printed $112/bbl in early April (vs $78 a year prior)
- EIA still forecasting Brent < $80/b by Q3 2026 — Iran resolution dependent

### Other Materials
- Softwood lumber: 10% tariff base, 25% on derivatives
- Cement (US): $96/MT (March 2026)
- Building materials prices broadly: +3.5% YoY (PPI, April 10)
- Copper wire, specialized valves, and concrete components leading the surge in MEP trades

## Downstream Impact (already showing up)
- **62% of builders reported suppliers raised material costs due to higher fuel prices** (April HMI)
- **36% of builders cut home prices in April** to maintain volume — margin compression visible
- **Project abandonments +22.8% MoM in March** (ConstructConnect Project Stress Index, +4.2%)
- Backlog rebounded to 8.6 months in March (ABC) — data center contractors at 10.6 months vs 8.3 without

## Impact on Building Materials
This is the dominant 2026 story for the sector. The combination of (1) Iran war oil shock, (2) Section 232 tariff escalation to 50% on metals, and (3) diesel fuel near-record jump is hitting input costs at the fastest pace since the 2022 supply-chain crisis. Producers with raw-material pricing power (CRH, Vulcan, Martin Marietta aggregates) are positioned to pass through; fabricators and finished-goods players face margin compression unless they have indexation in contracts. AGC has urged contract updates to reflect new tariff regime.
