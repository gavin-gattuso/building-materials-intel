- NEPA review delays
- Labor shortages constrain execution capacity (see *Labor Dynamics*)
- Tariff cost shock (see *Material & Energy Costs*) is now a third constraint — projects bid before April 2026 face squeezed margins on cement, steel rebar, asphalt

## Adjacent Signal: Data Center Infrastructure (Private)
- Data center contractors: 10.6 months backlog (vs 8.3 months without) — ABC March
- AI infrastructure buildout remains the strongest private-side driver of heavy-materials demand, partially offsetting residential softness

## Impact on Building Materials
Public infrastructure spending remains the strongest demand driver for heavy materials (cement, aggregates, steel rebar, asphalt). Peak disbursement in 2026 supports volumes even if residential weakens. Combined with private data center buildouts, the "non-residential heavy" end-market is the cleanest place to be exposed in 2026. Beneficiaries: Vulcan Materials, Martin Marietta, CRH, Eagle Materials, US Concrete.
