# Credit Availability & Lending Standards

## Signal Change (2026-05-12)
**Updated from "stable" to "tightening."** April–May KB evidence points to deteriorating credit conditions in construction-exposed financing, driven by the oil/tariff cost shock cascading into project economics. Bank lending surveys have not yet caught up to the on-the-ground stress signals.

## Current State (Q4 2025 SLOOS — most recent formal survey)
- C&I loans: 5-10% of banks tightened (modest)
- CRE (multifamily): modest easing
- CRE (construction/land dev): unchanged overall
- Divergence: large banks eased, smaller banks tightened
- CRE demand: stronger overall

## What's Changed Since Last Refresh

### Project-Level Credit Stress
- **ConstructConnect Project Stress Index +4.2% MoM in March** (Apr 11)
- **Project abandonments +22.8% MoM in March** — Iran war + oil shock disrupting underwriting assumptions
- Construction Dive (Apr 11): "Construction economic outlook is increasingly cloudy beyond data centers"

### Mortgage / Consumer Credit
- **LendingTree stock -22% on May 12** — Q1 2026 earnings disappointed despite GAAP turn to black and double-digit revenue growth; market signaling skepticism on consumer credit demand quality
- Mortgage application demand subdued (MBA, Apr 9) — higher rates plus economic uncertainty
- Refinance activity remains weak

### Builder Behavior as Credit Proxy
- **36% of builders cut home prices in April** — margin sacrifice to move inventory rather than carry financing cost
- HMI cut to 34 in April (lowest since Sep 2025) — economic uncertainty cited

## 2026 Outlook
- Banks expected standards to remain unchanged at year-end 2025; project-level stress now suggests Q2 SLOOS likely shows tightening
- Demand was expected to strengthen; April price cuts argue otherwise
- **Watch:** Next SLOOS release (expected May 2026) — first formal confirmation of tightening

## Impact on Building Materials
Tightening credit conditions, particularly for smaller residential and mid-sized commercial projects, will further bifurcate the demand picture. Large/data center/IIJA-funded projects retain financing access; mid-market private CRE and speculative residential face cooling credit. This reinforces the conclusion in *Demand Visibility*: heavy-materials with infrastructure/data center exposure outperform residential-leveraged peers.
