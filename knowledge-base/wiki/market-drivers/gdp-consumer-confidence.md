# GDP Growth & Consumer Confidence

## Consumer Confidence — April Update
- **NAHB HMI (April): 34, down 4 points, lowest since September 2025**
- **NAHB Remodeling Market Sentiment edged down in Q1 but remains positive** (Apr 14)
- Economic uncertainty + material cost surge cited as drivers
- 62% of builders reported supplier cost pass-through tied to fuel
- 36% of builders cut home prices in April (margin sacrifice)

> **Carried forward (March CCI):** Overall CCI 91.8, Present Situation 123.3, Expectations 70.9 — still showing better current conditions, weaker forward outlook. May Conference Board release not yet in KB.

## GDP Forecasts for 2026 (carried from April baseline)
- RSM: 2.2% growth
- Goldman Sachs: 2.8% growth (tariff fade + tax cuts via "One Big Beautiful Bill Act")
- **Risk to forecasts:** Tariff escalation to 50% on metals + Iran-driven oil shock were NOT in baseline assumptions when these forecasts were issued. Watch for downward revisions.

## Recession Probability
- Carried: 30% (down from 40% earlier in cycle)
- Directionally favorable but elevated; oil + tariff shock argue for upward revision

## GDP Reporting Pipeline
- BEA Q4 2025 GDP third estimate: scheduled Apr 9, 2026 (KB has the schedule, awaiting capture of release print)
- **Recommendation:** add BEA release-day scrape so Q1 2026 advance estimate (due late April) gets captured.

## Consumer Spending Pattern (carried)
- Focused on "cheap thrills" and necessities
- Moving away from expensive discretionary purchases
- Inflation expectations rising due to tariff passthrough and oil prices — now validated by Q1 input price prints

## Impact on Building Materials
GDP growth in the 2-3% band supports overall construction activity but does not unlock pent-up residential demand on its own. Consumer confidence weakness flows directly into:
- Big-ticket remodeling (Lowe's, Home Depot pro/DIY mix)
- Builder confidence and home-price discounting
- Discretionary product mix (premium fixtures, finishes — Masco, Fortune Brands, Kohler)

The forward signal from NAHB (HMI -4, Remodeling Sentiment down) is more reliable than the trailing CCI print for the building materials thesis.
